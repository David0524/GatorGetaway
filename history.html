The history of the home

Standing in the Duck Pond since 1899

Seven years before the University of Florida opened its Gainesville campus, this house was already here.

Built in 1899

When this house went up, Gainesville was a county seat and railroad town of a few thousand people. There was no university yet. The Buckman Act of 1905 created the University of Florida in Gainesville, and the school opened its new campus here in September 1906.

So the house has seen the whole story: the first students arriving, the town growing up around the campus, and every Gator season since.

Our hope is to become your home away from home throughout your Gator's years at UF.

We bought the house in 2026 to share it with Gator families. We're keeping as much of its character as we can, and adding the comforts a modern stay needs.

— Craig and Jenn

Then and now

Put the early photograph next to the house today and you can see what has lasted and what has changed.

Still here

  • The wraparound porch, with its gabled corner roof
  • Turned porch posts and a decorative frieze under the porch roof
  • Clapboard siding and the tall second story
  • A picket fence, though today it runs along the side yard

Since changed

  • A second-floor balcony once sat above the porch
  • Louvered shutters framed the windows
  • The picket fence ran along the street

The Duck Pond

The house sits in the Duck Pond, one of Gainesville's oldest neighborhoods, just northeast of downtown. It's a neighborhood of big shade trees, Victorian and early twentieth-century homes, and the pond it's named for.

In 1980 the neighborhood was listed on the National Register of Historic Places as the Northeast Gainesville Residential District, recognizing more than two hundred of its historic buildings.

The people who lived here
